Mars Missions

Introduction

Mars is the fourth planet from the sun. NASA has sent over 20 missions to Mars. Eventually, we plan on landing on the planet and eventually creating a permanent base on Mars.

The Mariner Era (1964–1971)

  • Mariner 3: Intended to take the first close-up photos of Mars during a flyby. Failed when its protective shroud did not open, trapping the spacecraft and draining its batteries.
  • Mariner 4: Executed the first successful flyby of Mars in July 1965. Succeeded by sending back 21 historic close-up images showing a heavily cratered, moon-like surface.
  • Mariner 6: Flew over the Martian equator in 1969 to study the atmosphere and surface. Succeeded by providing higher-resolution images and recording extreme surface temperatures.
  • Mariner 7: Flew over the southern polar region just days after Mariner 6. Succeeded by mapping the polar caps and proving they are mostly carbon dioxide ice.
  • Mariner 8 (1971): Failed during launch.
  • Mariner 9 (1971): Became the first spacecraft to successfully orbit Mars, mapping 85% of the planet.

Landers and Global Mapping (1975–2000)

  • Viking 1 & 2 (1975): Consisted of orbiters and the first successful NASA landers on the Martian surface.
  • Mars Observer (1992): Lost contact just before orbital insertion.
  • Mars Global Surveyor (1996): Mapped the entire Martian surface from orbit for over a decade.
  • Mars Pathfinder (1996): Delivered the stationary lander and the tiny Sojourner rover, the first wheel-based vehicle on Mars.
  • Mars Climate Orbiter (1998): Destroyed due to a navigation metric software error.
  • Mars Polar Lander / Deep Space 2 (1999): Lost communications during descent/impact.

Golden Age of Rovers and Orbiters (2001–2018)

  • Mars Odyssey (2001): An active orbiter mapping chemical elements and serving as a communications relay.
  • Spirit & Opportunity (2003): Twin Mars Exploration Rovers that vastly outlived their original 90-day designs.
  • Spirit & Opportunity (2003): Twin Mars Exploration Rovers that vastly outlived their original 90-day designs.
  • Phoenix (2007): Landed in the Martian arctic and confirmed subsurface water ice.
  • Curiosity (2011): Car-sized rover exploring Gale Crater, still actively searching for past environmental conditions suited for microbial life.
  • MAVEN (2013): Orbiter studying how Mars lost its atmosphere to space.
  • InSight (2018): Stationary geophysical lander that measured Mars' deep interior (quakes and core).
  • MarCO A & B (2018): Twin CubeSats that provided relay support during InSight's landing.

Recent and Ongoing Exploration (2020–Present)

  • Perseverance (2020): Advanced rover exploring Jezero Crater to cache rock samples for a future return journey.
  • Ingenuity (2020): Historic technology-demonstration helicopter that completed dozens of powered flights before concluding operations.

Upcoming Missions

  • ESCAPADE (Launched 2025): Twin active orbiters currently loitering near Earth's L2 point before a 2026 Mars trajectory injection.
